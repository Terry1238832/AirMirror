This policy covers the Mac and iPad App Store editions of AirMirror. Wireless screen sharing starts only after your confirmation and uses an encrypted local network connection to your paired Mac. The app does not send screen content to the developer and has no cloud accounts, advertising or analytics services.

The iPad camera is used only when you choose QR pairing. Camera images are not saved or uploaded. On Mac, the system camera interface reads the screen of an iPhone or iPad connected by USB. Audio input permission is requested only when you enable device sound.

Pairing credentials stay in the local device Keychain. Language, display name, random device identifier and connection preferences are stored locally. The app and sharing extension exchange only necessary destination, language and status data through their shared container. The receiver name and random identifier are visible on the local network.

Video, audio and window previews are processed in memory for the current feature. The app saves no recordings or screenshot history. On Mac, WindowPaste writes your selected screenshot to the system clipboard for you to paste manually. The system or other apps may sync and retain clipboard content.

You can stop sharing, unpair devices, revoke permissions or disable launch at login in system settings. Unpairing removes credentials on that device; an offline peer may need to be unpaired separately. Quitting releases live images but does not clear the clipboard. Deleting the app does not guarantee removal of Keychain items, preferences or data retained by other software.

For support or privacy assistance, contact terryappledev@gmail.com. Emails you choose to send are used only to handle your feedback; you may request deletion of copies held by the developer. Email services retain data under their own policies. Do not send pairing keys or screenshots containing private information.

App updates are provided by the App Store. This policy is updated when data handling changes. When you visit the online policy or support pages, your browser and the website host may process technical information needed for normal access.
